Talk 1 of 4

Bradley Harris

Senior Director of Product Design

Spend saved time on better judgment.

  • He started with almost no coding experience.
  • He built a vision prototype first.
  • It became the team's north star.
  • Then he wrote design standards into AI skills.

50%

less time spent on early feedback before critique

“This vision gave people something very specific to respond to…”

Bradley

“…Sometimes you'll run a critique for an AI skill and it'll rate your visuals like a 10 out of 10. And that subjectivity is what's missing.”

Bradley

“…What we do with that time is ultimately going to determine if we produce more stuff or we spend more time developing better judgment.”

Bradley

How to build a design skill

  1. Start with something you explain often.
  2. Pick a task with a clear purpose.
  3. Explain the reasoning behind it.
  4. Show good and bad examples.
  5. Run it, judge it, iterate.

Talk 2 of 4

Kseniia Lobko

Senior Product Designer, Cards team

Code is a new design material.

  • She kept putting off AI prototyping.
  • A deadline finally got her started.
  • Her first result was ugly, but clickable.
  • Now she prototypes first, then uses Figma.

“…I realized that I wasn't learning to code, but I was getting a new material to design in.”

Kseniia

“…Sometimes static screens can't show everything. There's no loading states, there's no error states…”

Kseniia

“…The prototype that works is not automatically a good prototype and not good design. So I'm still final decision maker.”

Kseniia

Her advice to herself, six months ago

  • Trust AI to help you start.
  • Treat it like a junior designer.
  • Connect your design system.
  • Give the problem, not the solution.
  • Critique it like a design review.

What she still owns

  • Deciding what to build.
  • Final documentation in Figma.
  • Judging the result.

Talk 3 of 4

Justin White

Product Designer

Make prototypes easy to see.

  • Designers once shared prototypes as zip files.
  • Nobody could see each other's work.
  • Now Cursor deploys straight to Vercel.
  • They skipped GitHub to lower friction.
  • Security rules shaped the tool choice.

“…There's a component of collaborative iteration there that felt a bit lost when we moved to AI.”

Justin

“…I think a lot of the friction was a beginner layer because it adds a lot of terminology and concepts…”

Justin

“So we initially thought customers were a little scared of AI, but we learned that all of them were fairly adept with it.”

Justin

What this unlocked

  • See each other's work, anytime.
  • Merge features across prototypes.
  • Test live prototypes with customers.
  • Go beyond the happy path.

A surprise from customer interviews

  • One customer started talking to the chat by voice.
  • Nobody had designed for that.

Talk 4 of 4

Michael Prelich

Design Engineer, leads design engineering

Go wide, not just deep.

  • An invite flow worked, but felt flat.
  • The team chose a lanyard metaphor.
  • No AI was used for the idea.
  • AI built each motion prototype in a day.
  • It became a pattern across the app.

1 day

per prototype, instead of up to two weeks

“…At the end of the day, the tool isn't going to come up with the idea for you.”

Michael

“…Having a good understanding of the underlying technology allowed me to express my idea in a way that the AI agent would understand…”

Michael

“If someone asks you to go try and make something spin, go try and make it spin and see what happens.”

Michael

“Limitations are creativity.”

Michael, quoting the design lead of Teenage Engineering

His three principles

  1. You are in charge of the idea.
  2. Learn the tools under the hood.
  3. Go wide, not just deep.

A Product Designer's Lens

From Evan Zhai, the author of this briefing

Evan Zhai

Senior Product Designer, health insurance

  • Real data reveals real edge cases.

    Kseniia and Justin are right about this.

  • But regulated industries can't always use it.

    In health insurance, member data needs strict approval. That's a difference in context, not capability.

  • So design the edge cases instead.

    Synthetic data can break things on purpose: long names, missing fields, denied claims.

  • Tools follow constraints, not trends.
